Physics in the Middle Years

Every Physics 40S student was once in a middle years classroom. This guide walks you through the physics in Manitoba's renewed Grade 7 and 8 science outcomes, and shows how each one leads to the four units of Physics 40S: mechanics, fields, electricity and medical physics.

For middle years teachers at any comfort level with physics. About 30 minutes. ★ marks the outcomes that matter most.

1. What changed

4 minutes

The renewed Kindergarten to Grade 10 science curriculum replaces the old cluster units with five strands that run all year. Physics no longer lives in one unit; it shows up across the year as gravity, energy, particles and light.

The renewed documents were posted for voluntary implementation in 2025–26, with a provincial rollout announced for fall 2026. Check your division's timeline.

Four threads to Physics 40S

Physics 40S has four units. Every activity in this guide carries a tag for the unit it builds toward.

MechanicsPhysics 40S Unit 1

Motion, forces, momentum, projectiles, circular motion, work and energy.

Before Grade 7: balanced forces, speed and inertia (Grade 3), friction and simple machines (Grade 5), Earth's gravity, normal force and buoyancy (Grade 6). Grade 7 adds weight in newtons.

FieldsPhysics 40S Unit 2

Exploration of space, low Earth orbit, and electric and magnetic fields.

Before Grade 7: forces that act at a distance, such as gravity, magnetism and static electricity (Grade 3), and Earth's gravity and the Solar System (Grade 6). Grade 7 adds gravity as a universal force, orbits, tides and space exploration.

ElectricityPhysics 40S Unit 3

Electric circuits and electromagnetic induction.

Before Grade 7: battery-powered circuits and electrical safety (Grade 6). Grades 7 and 8 build energy transfer and solar energy; circuits return in Grade 9.

Medical physicsPhysics 40S Unit 4

Radioactive decay, radiation and the body, imaging and treatment.

Before Grade 7: light as energy that travels and is reflected, absorbed or transmitted (Grade 4). Grade 7 adds thermal radiation, Grade 8 the electromagnetic spectrum.

2. Grade 7 physics

10 minutes

Grade 7 physics is about invisible influences: gravity pulling across empty space, and energy moving from place to place, mostly as heat.

Major themes

★ Gravity and weight

SCI.7.E.4SCI.7.E.5SCI.7.E.6SCI.7.E.7

Gravity is a universal pull between masses; weight is that pull near Earth, in newtons. Orbits and tides are gravity at work.

★ Energy moves and spreads out

SCI.7.E.8–E.11

Heat moves by conduction, convection and radiation. Energy makes things happen and always ends up partly as heat.

Particles in motion

SCI.7.E.1–E.3

Heating speeds particles up. That speed is kinetic energy.

The sky as a physics lab

SCI.7.E.12–E.16

The Solar System, apparent motion from Earth's rotation, seasons.

The physics in plain words

Mass is not weight

Mass (kg) is how much matter something has. Weight (N) is gravity's pull on it: about 9.8 N per kilogram on Earth, 1.6 N on the Moon (NASA).

An orbit is a fall that misses

A satellite falls toward Earth the whole time, but moves sideways so fast that the ground curves away beneath it. Gravity never switches off in space.

Heat and temperature differ

Temperature is how fast particles move on average. Heat is energy flowing from hot to cold. Cold doesn't flow in; heat flows out.

Energy is never used up

Energy moves between stores (motion, height, warmth, a stretched spring) and some always ends up as heat. Efficiency is the useful share.

Where does electricity show up in our day?

SCI.7.E.11SCI.7.C.1

Gravity simulation

The scientific method: a fair test

An insulation challenge (keep an ice cube frozen for 30 minutes) makes SCI.7.D.2 concrete.

Question
Which material keeps an ice cube frozen the longest?
Hypothesis
If we wrap the cup in wool, less ice will melt, because wool traps air and air conducts heat poorly.
Change
The wrapping material (independent variable).
Measure
Mass of meltwater after 30 minutes (dependent variable).
Keep the same
Ice cube size, cup, time, where the cups sit (controlled variables).
Conclude
Compare with the hypothesis and name sources of error.

Does a bathroom scale measure mass?

SCI.7.E.5

The scale shows kilograms, so it must measure mass. Students test the claim with evidence: the scale squeezes a spring, which responds to force, and on the Moon it would show about one-sixth the reading.

If you stood on the scale in an elevator that starts moving up, would the reading change? Why?

Physics hiding in other Grade 7 outcomes

  • Food chains are energy accounting SCI.7.E.19: only a small share of energy passes to each trophic level; the rest is dissipated as heat, exactly as in SCI.7.E.11.
  • Photosynthesis is an energy transfer SCI.7.E.18: light energy from the Sun is stored as chemical energy in plants.

Watch for

  • "Heavier objects fall faster." Challenge it with the drop race.
  • Fields "There's no gravity in space." Challenge it with orbits as falling: at the space station's height, gravity is still about 90 percent as strong as on the ground; astronauts float because they're in free fall around Earth (NASA).
  • Medical physics "Radiation means something radioactive." In Grade 7, radiation is heat from the Sun or a campfire travelling as electromagnetic waves. The ionizing radiation used in medicine comes in Physics 40S. That difference makes a great curiosity question.

3. Grade 8 physics

10 minutes

Grade 8 physics asks how the particles in a material decide how it behaves, and how the Sun's energy reaches Earth as electromagnetic radiation and what it does when it arrives.

Major themes

★ Density and the particle model

SCI.8.E.1SCI.8.E.2

d = m/V is many students' first formula to rearrange. Warm fluids are less dense, so they rise.

Fluids and pressure

SCI.8.E.3–E.5

Viscosity, and how temperature, volume and pressure are linked.

★ The electromagnetic spectrum

SCI.8.E.8SCI.8.E.9

Visible light is one slice of a spectrum from radio waves to X-rays. Its applications, from solar cells to medical X-rays, lead to two Physics 40S units.

★ Earth's energy budget

SCI.8.E.7SCI.8.E.13SCI.8.E.15SCI.8.E.17

Sunlight is absorbed or reflected (albedo) and eventually radiates back to space.

The physics in plain words

Density decides floating

Water is 1.00 g/cm³ and ice about 0.92 g/cm³, so ice floats. Things float when their overall density is less than the fluid's, not because they are "light".

Pressure is particles hitting walls

Heat a gas in a closed space and its particles hit the walls harder and more often, so pressure rises.

One spectrum, many names

Radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray and gamma are all electromagnetic waves. Toward gamma: shorter wavelength, higher frequency, more energy.

Albedo

The share of sunlight a surface reflects. Fresh snow reflects most of it; open water and dark soil absorb most of it and warm up.

DemonstrationMechanics

The Cartesian diver

SCI.8.E.1SCI.8.E.4

Float a medicine dropper (or a ketchup packet) in a full, capped 2 L bottle of water. Squeeze the bottle and the diver sinks; let go and it rises. A gentle squeeze makes it hover.

Nothing touched the diver. What changed that made it sink?

Squeezing compresses the air bubble inside the dropper, so the diver's overall density rises above the water's. Students met buoyancy as a force opposing gravity in Grade 6 (SCI.6.E.2, E.3).

How-to: Making a pipette or condiment-packet diver (TeachEngineering)

LabFields

Make an electromagnet

SCI.8.C.3SCI.9.E.11

An enrichment bridge: electromagnets are a Grade 9 outcome. It builds on Grade 6 circuits and the Grade 8 big idea that energy moves between stores.

Students wrap insulated copper wire around an iron nail and connect the ends to a battery. Current flowing through the coil makes a magnetic field that magnetizes the nail, so it picks up paper clips. Energy moves from chemical (battery) to electrical (wire), and some is lost as heat.

  • Question: can a nonmagnetic piece of metal be made magnetic?
  • Count the paper clips picked up; try more coils, or none.

Safety: The coil heats up quickly. Use one 1.5 V cell, connect it for a few seconds at a time, and wear safety glasses.

The scientific method: a fair test

Medical physics Can we protect ourselves from UV radiation? Students compare UV-sensitive beads or cards in sunlight and behind different UV-blocking materials SCI.8.D.2SCI.8.C.3.

Question
Which material reduces the colour change of UV-sensitive beads the most?
Hypothesis
Materials that block more UV radiation will reduce the beads' colour change.
Change
The material covering the beads (independent variable).
Measure
Colour change, using a consistent rating scale or photographs (dependent variable).
Keep the same
Exposure time, sunlight conditions, and the type of bead or card (controlled variables).
Conclude
Use the results to make evidence-based sun-safety recommendations and discuss the limitations of the model.

Grade 7

Electricity Demo: a small solar-powered device

Run a low-voltage motor or buzzer from a small classroom solar cell under a lamp or sunlight, then shade it. Students name the input and output energy and predict how distance or angle will change the output. Does brighter light always produce a larger output? What would we need to control to test this?

Electricity Lab: testing a small solar cell

Keep the same cell and lamp; change one variable (distance or angle), record the voltage or another teacher-approved output, and repeat trials. Use a multimeter only if students have been trained on it. A bridge activity: solar radiation itself is a Grade 8 outcome.

Mechanics Lab: weight versus mass SCI.7.E.5SCI.7.C.2

Groups hang known masses from a spring scale reading in newtons, then graph weight against mass. The slope comes out near 9.8 N per kg. Extension: find each weight on the Moon, where gravity is 1.62 N/kg (NASA Moon Fact Sheet). That slope is g, used in every Physics 40S force problem (F = mg).

Indigenous connection: the iglu and heat transfer SCI.7.A.1SCI.7.E.9

Hard snow walls are good insulators that keep in body heat and the heat from oil lamps. Some iglus have tiered floors so people can sleep higher up, where it's warmer, and narrow entry passages are sometimes dug just below ground level. Using convection, explain why sleeping higher is warmer. Why might a low entrance help?

Source: "Igloo," The Canadian Encyclopedia

Mechanics Indigenous connection: the bow as an energy store SCI.7.A.1SCI.7.E.11

Drawing a bow stores elastic energy in the bent wood, and releasing it moves that energy to the arrow. In Montana, a physics class studied elastic potential energy using two traditional bows made by Francis Cahoon of the Confederated Salish and Kootenai Tribes, hanging weights from the strings to measure how the bows bend. For a Manitoba class, invite a local bowmaker or Knowledge Keeper through your division's protocols. Where does the energy in a flying arrow come from, and where does it end up? Leads to work and energy in Physics 40S.

Grade 8

Mechanics Lab: density from a graph SCI.8.E.1SCI.8.C.2

Groups measure the mass and volume (by water displacement) of several different-sized pieces of one material, such as modelling clay or aluminum, and graph mass against volume. The slope is the density; a second material gives a different slope. Leads to reading slopes in Physics 40S kinematics: the slope of a velocity–time graph is acceleration.

Electricity Lab: solar-cell output investigation SCI.8.E.7SCI.8.C.3

Students change the angle or distance of a small solar cell from a lamp, control the other variables, take at least three readings per condition, graph them, and write a conclusion that refers to the data. Use low-voltage equipment and a teacher-approved light source. Connects solar energy to generating electricity.

Electricity Demo: conductors and insulators

With a low-voltage battery and a lamp or LED, leave a gap in a complete circuit and place test materials in it. The test is whether the material completes the electrical path, not whether it is "good" or "bad". Why might a material be a useful insulator in one context and a conductor in another? Reviews Grade 6 circuits and conductors SCI.6.E.6 and bridges to Grade 9 SCI.9.E.10; never use wall outlets.

Electricity Lab: build and troubleshoot a simple circuit

Students draw a labelled circuit diagram, build it with a battery pack, wires and a bulb or LED, then introduce one fault at a time (a loose wire, an open switch, a missing connection) and explain its effect. How can a diagram help another person reproduce your circuit? Builds on Grade 6 circuits SCI.6.E.6 and leads to building circuits from schematics in Physics 40S. Avoid short-circuiting the battery.

Science 9–10 and senior high

Medical physics Induced genetic mutations SCI.9.E.19

Students have met UV and X-rays in Grade 8. This is the moment to introduce the idea that certain types of radiation can affect biological tissue, which Physics 40S develops into the effects of ionizing and non-ionizing radiation and their use in diagnosis and treatment.

Medical physics Nuclear fusion and energy from stars SCI.10.E.17

Students build a concept map linking nuclear fusion, energy released by stars, solar radiation and the electromagnetic spectrum. Point out that fusion in stars is not the same process as radioactive decay, the heart of Physics 40S medical physics.

Electricity How does Manitoba get electricity to our homes? SCI.9.E.12–E.13

Trace the path from water behind a dam to a turbine, generator, transmission lines and household devices. Name the energy changes, plus one benefit and one trade-off of hydroelectric generation. Leads to generators and electromagnetic induction in Physics 40S.

Electricity Why are copper and other metals used in wires? SCI.10.E.2

Compare conductors and insulators and connect material choice to electron arrangement. A conceptual bridge only: current, resistance and circuit calculations belong to Grade 9 and Physics 40S.

Electricity What is the full cost of a battery? SCI.10.E.13SCI.10.E.22

Map the materials, purpose, and impacts from extraction to disposal of one battery-powered technology, then propose one evidence-based improvement such as longer device life or better recycling.

Electricity Senior high: design a two-lamp circuit Physics 40S

Design a battery circuit where one lamp can be switched off without turning off the other. Draw the schematic, predict current paths, and at the senior level calculate equivalent resistance. What would happen to the other lamp if one failed?

Electricity Senior high: a moving magnet and a coil Physics 40S

Move a bar magnet into and out of a coil connected to a meter (or use an approved simulation). Change the speed and direction and connect the results to generators and changing magnetic flux.
