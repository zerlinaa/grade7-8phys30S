Physics Starts in Grade 7 and 8

Every Physics 40S student was once in a middle years classroom. This guide walks you through the physics in Manitoba's renewed Grade 7 and 8 science outcomes, with demos, labs and activities that build the foundations senior high physics relies on.

For middle years teachers at any comfort level with physics. About 30 minutes. ★ marks the outcomes that matter most.

1. What changed

3 minutes

The renewed Kindergarten to Grade 10 science curriculum replaces the old cluster units with five strands that run all year. Physics no longer lives in one unit; it shows up across the year as gravity, energy, particles and light.

The renewed documents were posted for voluntary implementation in 2025–26, with a provincial rollout announced for fall 2026. Check your division's timeline.

2. Grade 7 physics

11 minutes

Grade 7 physics is about invisible influences: gravity pulling across empty space, and energy moving from place to place, mostly as heat.

Major themes

★ Gravity and weight

SCI.7.E.4SCI.7.E.5SCI.7.E.6SCI.7.E.7

Gravity is a universal pull between masses; weight is that pull near Earth, in newtons. Orbits and tides are gravity at work.

★ Energy moves and spreads out

SCI.7.E.8–E.11

Heat moves by conduction, convection and radiation. Energy makes things happen and always ends up partly as heat.

Particles in motion

SCI.7.E.1–E.3

Heating speeds particles up. That speed is kinetic energy.

The sky as a physics lab

SCI.7.E.12–E.16

The Solar System, apparent motion from Earth's rotation, seasons.

Demonstrate an understanding of the particle theory of matter and its significance to understanding the properties and behaviours of substances in different states of matter.
SCI.7.E.2
Demonstrate an understanding of how adding or removing energy affects the speed, and therefore, the kinetic energy of particles in matter.Include: temperature change, state change, conservation of mass during state change, freezing, melting, evaporation, condensation, sublimation.
SCI.7.E.3
Demonstrate the understanding that boiling and melting points are different in different substances due to variations in attraction between the particles that make up each substance.

Demonstrate the understanding that gravity is a universal attractive force between objects, extends infinitely, and increases with the masses of the objects.

Demonstrate the understanding that the gravitational force on objects on Earth is observed as a downward force on the object called weight.Include: mass, newton (N), weight differences on the Moon or other planets.
SCI.7.E.6
Demonstrate an understanding of the role of gravity in the orbits of planets and various satellites.

Demonstrate the understanding that tides result from the gravitational interaction between the Moon and large bodies of water.
SCI.7.E.8
Demonstrate the understanding that when an object is heated, it has more energy than when it is cold.

SCI.7.E.10
Demonstrate the understanding that many phenomena can be understood in terms of energy exchanges.
★ SCI.7.E.11
Demonstrate the understanding that when energy is transferred from a store, it can make things happen, and energy is ultimately dissipated to the environment as heat in the process.Include: efficiency.

The scale and structure of the Solar System; apparent motion of the Sun, Moon and stars caused by Earth's rotation; motion of planets against background stars; human space exploration; the astronomical significance of latitude and longitude (including seasons).

Measure using the appropriate techniques, tools, and units.Physics-related items include spring scale, speed (km/h, m/s), force (N) and energy (J).

The physics in plain words

Mass is not weight

Mass (kg) is how much matter something has. Weight (N) is gravity's pull on it: about 9.8 N per kilogram on Earth, 1.6 N on the Moon.

An orbit is a fall that misses

A satellite falls toward Earth the whole time, but moves sideways so fast that the ground curves away beneath it. Gravity never switches off in space.

Heat and temperature differ

Temperature is how fast particles move on average. Heat is energy flowing from hot to cold. Cold doesn't flow in; heat flows out.

Energy is never used up

Energy moves between stores (motion, height, warmth, a stretched spring) and some always ends up as heat. Efficiency is the useful share.

Physics hiding in other Grade 7 outcomes

  • Food chains are energy accounting SCI.7.E.19: only a small share of energy passes to each trophic level; the rest is dissipated as heat, exactly as in SCI.7.E.11.
  • Photosynthesis is an energy transfer SCI.7.E.18: light energy from the Sun is stored as chemical energy in plants.

Watch for

  • "Heavier objects fall faster." Challenge it with the drop race.
  • "There's no gravity in space." Challenge it with orbits as falling.
